AI in engineering and manufacturing

Not every problem needs AI, and pretending otherwise is how projects fail.

The right approach: where a deterministic, programmable solution exists and produces exact results, that solution wins. AI earns its place only where an exact programmatic solution does not exist or is cost-prohibitive to build. In engineering and manufacturing especially, a probabilistic error is expensive: a single wrong value in a production record can block an entire manufacturing run.

Applied well, this looks like three tiers of capability rather than AI layered on everything at once.

Systems integration & data automation.

Design systems, planning systems, and document platforms connected into a single, deterministic pipeline: structured data moved automatically and accurately between systems, with a full audit trail and clean rollback if any step fails. AI is introduced only at the edges where it adds genuine value, such as document intelligence on incoming change requests, kept fully isolated from the deterministic core so a model's uncertainty never touches production data.

Intelligent design & search.

Visual and geometric search across historical design records, so a rough sketch or partial spec instantly surfaces the closest existing designs instead of starting from scratch. Automated classification and metadata tagging, combining language and vision models to clean up and organise design data at scale. Automated identification of lower-cost, off-the-shelf substitutes for custom-designed components.

Predictive manufacturing intelligence.

Real-time cost estimation as a product is being designed, based on geometry, material, and current production rates. Predictive flags for manufacturability issues before a design ever reaches the production floor. Fast surrogate models that give instant early-stage estimates ahead of expensive full simulations.

This same class of model extends naturally into computer-vision-driven cost and quality assessment: analysing design or product images directly to calculate pricing factors or flag defects, a task otherwise done manually and inconsistently.
